How to track unpaid invoicesin Google Sheets.

The short answer: keep one row per invoice with its date, due date, amount and amount paid, let a formula set the status, and review an aging report every week.

The five columns every invoice log needs

  • Invoice number and client name, so every payment can be matched.
  • Issue date and due date. Most small businesses use 14 or 30 days.
  • Invoice total, including tax and discounts.
  • Amount paid so far, so part-payments are visible.
  • Balance, calculated as total minus amount paid.

Let a formula set the status

Typing "paid" by hand is how invoices get forgotten. Instead, a status column can check the numbers: if the balance is zero the invoice is Paid, if something was paid it is Partial, if the due date has passed it is Overdue, and otherwise it is Unpaid. In Google Sheets this is one IFS formula copied down the column.

Use an aging report to decide who to call

An aging report groups what you are owed by how late it is: 1 to 30 days, 31 to 60 days and 90+ days. The older the invoice, the less likely it is to be paid, so call the 60+ group first.

A 10-minute weekly routine

  • Every Monday, record every payment that arrived.
  • Filter the status column to Overdue.
  • Send a polite reminder to every overdue client, starting with the oldest.
  • Check the dashboard total for money outstanding and compare it with last week.
